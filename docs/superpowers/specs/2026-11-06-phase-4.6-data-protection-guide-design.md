# Phase 4.6 Design — Data protection guide

**Date:** 2026-11-06
**Phase:** 4.6
**Surface:** Docs
**Stage:** phase (one phase, implementation-ready)
**Status:** approved 2026-11-06

**Builds on:** Phase 4.5 — `docs/concepts/overview.md`, which the guide's start-here table may link. Phase 4.4 — nothing in the API changes here.

## Goal

A deployer who must answer to the GDPR can open one page, find each duty they carry, and see which Mnemosyne feature supports it, where the mechanics are documented, and what the library does not do. A short section says where the EU AI Act does and does not apply. This closes the milestone 4 criterion "The data protection guide is published".

## Why

The facts exist but are spread over `data-lifecycle.md` (erasure, retention, review), `scopes-and-subjects.md` (isolation, subjects), `telemetry.md` (what signals never carry), `capture-and-review.md` (the guard and redaction) and `postgresql.md` (what erasure leaves on disk). A deployer starts from a duty, not from a feature. Nothing says what Mnemosyne cannot do for them.

## Approaches considered

| Decision | Choice | Rejected |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | **By deployer duty, mapped to features.** A table of duties against the feature that supports each, with a link to the page holding the mechanics. The mechanics stay where they are, so each fact has one home. By the user. | By feature with GDPR notes (the reader has to search); a new home that moves the facts (large edit, strips the other pages of their local explanation). |
| Limits | **A "What Mnemosyne does not do" section and a not-legal-advice note.** Mnemosyne is a building block; the deployer stays responsible. Each gap carries what to do about it. By the user. | Inline mentions only (easy to miss); leaving limits out. |
| AI Act | **A short section.** Mnemosyne is a memory library, not an AI system or a model, so provider duties do not attach to it directly. The deployer's system may fall under the Act, and provenance, the review gate and telemetry support logging and oversight. It points to the regulation and claims no classification. By the user. | Skipping it; an article-by-article mapping (needs legal review this repo cannot give, and ages). |

## The page

`docs/guides/data-protection.md`, `sidebar_position: 10` (after the guides numbered 1 to 9). Concrete first, limits last, in the repository's style.

1. **What this page is.** Engineering guidance, not legal advice. Mnemosyne is a library inside the deployer's application; the deployer decides purposes and bases and carries the duties.
2. **What data Mnemosyne holds.** One short table: the question text and its embedding, the graph (steps and tool names), tags, provenance (who or what produced it, optionally a subject id), runs, outcomes and feedback, review state, counters. States which of these can contain personal data and where it comes from (the question and the graph, mostly).
3. **Duty to feature.** A table with one row per duty, each linking the mechanics:
   - *Erasure on request (Art. 17):* `EraseAsync` by scope or subject; one transaction; what it removes and what it keeps.
   - *Data minimisation and privacy by design (Art. 5(1)(c), 25):* the capture guard and redaction hook, the Sentinel adapter, the review gate that holds a capture back until a person approves.
   - *Storage limitation (Art. 5(1)(e)):* the retention policy and prune.
   - *Purpose and tenant separation:* scopes; subjects.
   - *Accountability and records (Art. 5(2), 30):* provenance, the receipt and run records, telemetry that carries no user data, the opaque ids to correlate on.
   - *Security of processing (Art. 32):* storage encryption and backups as a storage concern; telemetry kept clean so exporters need no special handling.
   - *Access and portability (Art. 15, 20):* what the library offers (listing and reading by scope and subject) and what it does not offer (no export format); the deployer builds the export.
4. **What Mnemosyne does not do.** Each item with what to do: data at rest after an erase (point to the PostgreSQL page); personal data inside an embedding and the question text (erase removes the row; an embedding model call already sent the text to that provider); the host's own chat model prompts and logs; backups and replicas; consent, lawful basis and notices; finding every place a person appears without a subject id; deciding the controller and processor roles.
5. **The EU AI Act in short.** Where it does not apply to the library, where it may apply to the deployer's system, and which Mnemosyne features help with record-keeping and human oversight. It links the regulation text and states no classification.
6. **A deployer checklist.** Set a subject on every capture that is about a person; turn on the guard; decide on the review gate; set retention; export telemetry without extra scrubbing; plan the erase-then-rotate-backups procedure; write down the roles.

Edits elsewhere: link the page from `docs/index.md`, `docs/concepts/scopes-and-subjects.md`, `docs/guides/data-lifecycle.md` and the 4.5 overview and host guide checklist.

## Tests

- `npm run build` with `onBrokenLinks` and `onBrokenAnchors` set to throw.
- A claim check: every statement about behaviour (what erase removes, what telemetry omits, retention rules) is checked against the page it links and, where it is a code fact, against `src/`. No claim exists on the new page that is not on, or checkable from, a linked page.
- Article numbers are checked against the regulation text before they are written, and any that cannot be confirmed are dropped.

## Anti-goals

- **Legal claims.** The page never says Mnemosyne "is GDPR compliant". It says what supports which duty.
- **Duplicated mechanics.** If a fact has a home page, the guide links it.
